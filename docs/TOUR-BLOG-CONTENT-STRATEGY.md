## Build content around traveler decisions

The goal is not to publish many thin posts. It is to answer useful questions and create an ethical path from the answer to the most relevant experience.

### Cluster A — choose a desert experience
- Guide: “Jaisalmer Desert Safari: Which Experience Fits Your Trip?”
- Comparison: same-evening camel safari, cultural evening, sleeping under stars, Swiss-tent overnight.
- Related pages: each tour's dedicated detail page.
- Main conversion: ask about current price, date, departure, route and inclusions.

### Cluster B — plan a short visit
- Guide: “Jaisalmer in 2 Days”
- Guide: “Jaisalmer in 3 Days”
- Link to the Fort guide, arrival guide, half-day/full-day city tours, and desert choices.
- Itinerary clocks must be indicative; leave operational buffer and mark seasonal activities as date-dependent.

### Cluster C — heritage and arrival
- Guide: “A Practical Guide to Staying Inside Jaisalmer Fort”
- Guide: “How to Reach Jaisalmer and Navigate the Final Fort Walk”
- Show the route/arrival constraint early; make expectations clear rather than surprising guests after they book.

### Cluster D — travel readiness
- Best time to visit
- Desert packing checklist
- Food, havelis, markets and crafts
- Beyond-the-Fort planning
- Add current sources, reviewed date and practical caveats; never imply affiliation or guaranteed access.

## Recommended tour detail page format

1. Hero: strong approved photograph, clear name, brief summary, one primary enquiry CTA.
2. At-a-glance row: time needed, overnight yes/no, type of journey and suitable traveller — only from verified records.
3. Narrative: describe the sequence and sense of place in concise, sensory but accurate language.
4. Timeline: each step has an order, time-of-day label if confirmed/approximate, title, details and an optional related photo. Use “timing confirmed for your date” in operator response, not fictional fixed times.
5. Included / not included: explicit separate lists. Do not collapse uncertain items into an apparent promise.
6. Practical notes: starting point, transport, physical effort, weather/season, what to bring, children/accessibility where known.
7. FAQs: price, duration, pickup, entry fees, language, weather, cancellation, transport, and what to confirm.
8. Related experiences, useful blog guide, then final CTA.
9. SEO: unique title/description, canonical, OG image with absolute URL, breadcrumbs, relevant structured data only if its claims are true.

## Draft-to-live publishing gates

- `draft`: authoring/preview only, never in public navigation or sitemap.
- `ready_for_review`: schema/path/assets and editorial QA pass.
- `approved_for_release`: owner has confirmed tour operations facts and photo rights.
- `live`: page generated and links, metadata, image loads, sitemap membership and mobile layout checked.

For articles, every material claim needs a credible source or must be removed/qualified. For tours, the content score cannot approve truth: the hotel operator must validate price, timings, duration, route, pickup, inclusion/exclusion, safety and availability.

## Campaign/content cadence

Recommended first batch after the content system is built:
- 3 experience pages: Sunset Camel Safari, Cultural Desert Safari and Swiss Tent Safari; choose them only after owner verifies current service details.
- 3 high-intent guides: Desert Safari Comparison, Jaisalmer 2-Day Itinerary and How to Reach / Fort Arrival.
- Next: Sleep Under the Stars, half-day/full-day city tour, 3-day itinerary, Fort guide and packing list.
- Continue from search and enquiry questions rather than publishing on a schedule just to fill the blog.

Measure only with verified analytics: clicks to a tour, WhatsApp/booking CTA clicks, engaged page views, enquiry messages attributable to a page, and broken-link/404 rates. Do not invent a traffic or conversion baseline.
